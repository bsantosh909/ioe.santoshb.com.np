## Syllabus

1. **Sociology, Society and Human-Environment Relationship** (4 hours)
   1. Sociology as the study of human society
   2. Scope of sociology to architectural studies
   3. Society as a human-environment system
   4. Concept of social architecture and spatial organization
2. **Social Structure, Institutions and Social Processes** (4 hours)
   1. Social structure in rural and urban Nepal
   2. Institutions: family, religion, economy, governance and their spatial roles
   3. Social groups, communities and neighborhood organization
   4. Social interaction: forms and implications for space
   5. Introduction to social change within institutions and communities
3. **Culture and Civilization** (6 hours)
   1. Concept of culture and civilization
   2. Culture, cultural drift, diffusion, transmission, acculturation
   3. Family and kinship systems
   4. Gender and power: patriarchal and matriarchal systems
   5. Tangible and intangible cultural heritage
   6. Civilization and archaeology: early city forms (Indus, Mesopotamia, Egypt)
   7. Nepali context: Lumbini archaeology and Buddhist urbanism as sacred city models
4. **Social and Cultural Dimensions of Architecture in Nepal** (6 hours)
   1. Socio-cultural meaning of open spaces: chowks, ghats, courtyards, public squares
   2. Socio-cultural meaning of built spaces: houses, temples, palaces, communal halls
   3. Ethnic and regional traditions: Newar settlements, Mithila houses, Tharu housing, Khasa settlements
   4. Rituals, festivals and community practices shaping architecture
5. **Social Change and Modernization** (4 hours)
   1. Processes of social change: migration, urbanization, globalization
   2. Impacts on traditional architecture and settlements
   3. Changing family, kinship and community patterns
   4. Contemporary socio-cultural challenges in Nepali architectural design
6. **Applied Study: Socio-Cultural Analysis** (6 hours)
   1. Application of sociological and cultural concepts to architecture
   2. Field observation of a settlement, neighborhood or building type
   3. Mapping social use of spaces
   4. Short reflective assignment linking social practices and spatial design
   5. Group and class presentations of applied findings
