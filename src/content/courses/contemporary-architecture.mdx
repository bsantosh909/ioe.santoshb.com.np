## Syllabus

1. **Defining Modern and Contemporary Architecture** (2 hours)
   1. Modern and contemporary architecture
   2. Architecture as evolving practice: global discourses and local responses
   3. Spectacle and substance; iconic and contextual approaches
   4. Contemporary Nepal: influence and stereotypes
2. **Early Modern Roots (Pre-1920 Foundations)** (5 hours)
   1. Industrial revolution: new building types (factories, exhibitions)
   2. Arts and crafts, art nouveau (Morris, Gaudi, Mackintosh, Loos)
   3. Chicago school pioneers (Burnham and Root, Jenney, Sullivan, Wright)
3. **High Modernism (1920 to 1970)** (12 hours)
   1. Werkbund and Bauhaus: Behrens, Gropius, Mies van der Rohe
   2. International style and interwar planning (CIAM, Weissenhof)
   3. Masters: Le Corbusier, Wright, Aalto, Kahn
   4. Post-WWII modernism: brutalism, metabolism, De Stijl legacy
   5. Early modernism in Nepal (1950s to 1970s): civic and institutional buildings
4. **Postmodernism and Pluralism (1970 to 1990)** (8 hours)
   1. Crisis of modernism and crisis of meaning
   2. Postmodernism: Venturi, Rossi, Graves, Johnson, Stirling
   3. Neo-rationalism, neo-functionalism
   4. Pluralism and early Nepali encounters with global idioms
5. **Deconstructivism, High-Tech and Globalization (1990 to 2010)** (8 hours)
   1. Deconstructivism: Eisenman, Hadid, Tschumi, Gehry, Koolhaas, Libeskind
   2. High-Tech: Foster, Rogers, Piano, Grimshaw
   3. Globalization: star-architecture versus context-driven practice
   4. Nepali architecture engaging with global discourse
6. **Contemporary Practice (2010 to Present)** (4 hours)
   1. Diversity: regionalism, identity, participatory and community design
   2. Ecology and sustainability: green building, biophilic design, passive strategies
   3. Technology: parametric design, smart materials, digital fabrication
   4. Global examples with Nepali case comparisons
7. **Contemporary Architecture of Nepal (Past to Present)** (6 hours)
   1. Trajectory from early modern influences to contemporary diversity
   2. Kathmandu Valley and beyond: civic, cultural, public projects
   3. Post-2015 earthquake reconstruction: heritage-sensitive practices
   4. Current tendencies: climate responsiveness, material craft, community engagement

## Practicals

Fifteen hours: seminar discussion on what makes architecture contemporary with global versus Nepali perspectives, comparative analysis of early modern and high modern works with Nepali parallels, and a field or virtual study of a recent Nepali public building with group presentation and seminar on post-earthquake reconstruction.
